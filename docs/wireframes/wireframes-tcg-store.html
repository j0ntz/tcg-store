Home

1 Search leads the page because most singles shoppers arrive knowing the card they want. Home swaps the header search for this larger one; every other page keeps search in the header.

2 New arrivals is the first product row. Returning buyers check what came in since their last visit.

3 Shop by set matches how collectors think about cards: set first, then card number.

4 The packing and returns promises answer the main worry about buying cards by mail. The short version also runs in the bar at the top of every page.

Shop: singles

1 Filters stay in a left column while the shopper scans results. Set and rarity sit near the top because they narrow results fastest. In stock only starts on.

2 Each tile shows set and number because one Pokemon has dozens of printings. The price is the cheapest condition in stock. Tiles have no add-to-cart button because a single needs a condition first.

3 Active filters show as removable chips above the results, so the shopper can see why the list looks the way it does.

Card: Umbreon ex

1 Photos show the exact copy for cards over $100. Open question: where should that price line sit?

2 Condition is the real decision on a single, so each row shows condition, price, and stock together.

3 Sold-out conditions stay listed with Notify me, so the page never dead-ends.

4 Open question: show a market price reference at all? It builds trust and also invites comparison shopping.

5 Shipping and packing details sit next to the buy button, where the worry about the card arriving safely comes up.

Sealed: Elite Trainer Box

1 Sealed product has one condition, so the purchase limit takes the condition picker's place. Limits keep new sets from going to resellers.

2 The contents list answers the most common question about sealed product before it gets asked.

3 Open question: take preorders for upcoming sets? That needs a release date on the page and a decision on when to charge the card.

Cart

1 Shipping status comes first: free shipping unlocked, or how much more unlocks it.

2 Every line repeats the condition, because the condition is part of what was bought.

3 Express pay sits under the main checkout button for buyers who would rather not type a card number.

4 Open question: hold a single when it goes in the cart, or only once checkout starts? Most singles are one of a kind.

Checkout

1 Checkout drops the store nav and search. There is one way forward and one way back to the cart.

2 Guest checkout on one page in four numbered steps; no account needed. Open question: ship outside the United States?

3 The envelope option stays visible but disabled above $20, so buyers learn it exists for small orders.

4 Card fields stand in for the payment provider's own form. Stripe or Square, not chosen yet.

5 The ZIP field shows the inline error pattern used across the whole form.

Order confirmed

1 Four steps set the delivery expectation and match the emails the buyer will get.

2 Creating an account is offered after the purchase, never required before it.
